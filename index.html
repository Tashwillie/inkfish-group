What the sector is dealing with

Four problems that keep meeting each other.

They are usually handled separately, by different people, in different budgets. Inkfish exists because they are the same problem seen from four sides.

01

Residential provision

Too few places, in the wrong places, at prices that are set by scarcity rather than by need.

02

Transitional housing

Young people leaving care with nowhere designed for the step between a placement and a tenancy.

03

The gap around fostering

Children who cannot be fostered and do not need an institution, with nothing built for them in between.

04

Local authority finances

Budgets absorbed by a small number of very high cost placements, squeezing everything else.

One group, three kinds of work.

Care, housing and education carry equal weight here. They are commissioned separately, budgeted separately and inspected separately. A child experiences them as one thing.

The programme

We are building thirty homes, one at a time.

Thirty small specialist homes, EBD and LD, rather than one large one, each opening on its own terms. The model does not change with scale, and the pace is set by the quality of the team we can recruit, not by the property we can buy.

Working with commissioners

We would rather start with the child than the vacancy.

Our starting point is the young person: the home they need, the education that helps them progress, and the support that prepares them for their next step. We welcome collaboration with Regional Care Cooperatives, local authorities and regional commissioning partnerships to shape provision around identified needs.

Evaluation

We would rather be measured than believed.

Children’s services carry decisions that are hard to reverse and easy to justify after the event. The discipline that prevents it is ordinary: decide what good looks like before a child arrives, measure it honestly, and let the evidence change what you do next.

Baseline before we start

A placement begins with a written baseline against the care plan and the education plan, so progress is measured from a known starting point rather than remembered afterwards.

The same measures every time

Measures are defined before a service opens, not chosen once the results are in. Stable measures make comparison possible across homes, across years and across providers.

Reported to the people who placed

Our commitment is to report progress to the placing authority on an agreed cycle, including, and especially, where a child is not progressing.

Open to scrutiny we do not control

We would rather an evaluation that can embarrass us than one that cannot. Where a commissioning partner wants independent or academic evaluation of a service, we will support it and share the data.

A sector that cannot measure itself cannot improve. We intend to be measurable.

Why one group

A child does not experience housing, care and education as separate services. We stopped running them as though they were.

Better outcomes

The building, the team and the education plan are designed against the same brief, rather than assembled after a child has arrived.

Lower cost

Owning the property and the operating model removes a layer of margin, and takes the pressure off placing a second child for the wrong reasons.

Clearer accountability

One group to hold to account, with each division named, registered where it must be, and answerable for its own part.

A personal note from Louis

I was 15 when I was placed in the home in this photograph. It has since been demolished, but I still remember what it felt like to arrive there and realise this was now supposed to be my home.

That experience has never left me. I can’t change it, but I can use it to help build something very different.

What I understand now is that where a child lives, who looks after them and what they learn are decided by different people, in different budgets, at different times. The child experiences them as one life.

That is why Inkfish is more than a care company. Care, housing and education sit together here because they belong together. But Inkfish isn’t about me. It’s about what we build together.

We want every home to feel like a home, every move towards independence to be planned rather than timed by a birthday, and every child’s education to be part of the plan from the start.

We can’t change what happened before a child reaches us.
But together, we can help change what happens next.

Louis Stedman-BryceFounder, Inkfish Group
